Linear discriminant analysis (LDA)

Compare class centers using one shared description of spread.

Start here

Imagine drawing an oval around each of two clouds of dots. Give both ovals the same shape and tilt, but put each at the center of its own cloud. LDA uses a straight boundary to separate the two groups in this picture.

Training means using past examples with known outcomes to find a pattern. A prediction applies that pattern to a new case. The lab uses simulated data so you can see what changes when you move one control.

Example, step by step

If late and on-time jobs are equally common, think of a new point as choosing the closer group after allowing for the common oval shape. If delays are rare, the model also accounts for that before deciding. The oval describes which differences are typical for the group.

What to keep in mind

Covariance describes how measurements spread and move together. A shared covariance means assuming the groups have the same pattern of spread even when their centers differ.

How it learns

LDA estimates a center for each class and pools their within-class covariance. Under the Gaussian model with shared covariance, squared terms in x cancel when comparing classes. The resulting boundary is a straight line in two dimensions. [1]

Equation and terms

The math behind the idea

δc(x) = xᵀΣ⁻¹μc − ½ μcᵀΣ⁻¹μc + ln πc

μc is the class center, Σ combines spread and correlations, and πc is the class prior. The largest δc wins. The lab adds a small diagonal term to stabilize inversion of Σ. [1] [2]

Try it in the lab

Move the white diamond across the border. Then increase the diagonal adjustment. The line can move or turn, but the model still uses one shared oval shape. Tiny steps on the plot come from drawing it on a grid.

How to read the chart

The sliders place the white diamond on the chart. Move one slider at a time. The horizontal axis shows open items and the vertical axis shows delivery delay. The controls express both measurements on a 0–1 scale, with 0 meaning low and 1 meaning high. The colored background tells you which label the model would assign at that spot.

Lab

Use and evaluate

Try LDA when the groups have roughly similar shapes. Compare with logistic regression on held-out data, and check whether rare classes are detected. [2]

Review

Why does the boundary stay straight when regularization changes?

Both classes still share the same covariance, so the quadratic terms still cancel.
