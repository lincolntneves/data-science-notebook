Linear support vector machine (SVM)

Find a dividing line with room on both sides.

Start here

Imagine drawing a line between two groups of dots and marking a buffer zone on both sides. You want a wide buffer, but some dots may fall inside it or cross to the wrong side. A linear SVM balances the width of that buffer with the cost of those cases.

Training means using past examples with known outcomes to find a pattern. A prediction applies that pattern to a new case. The lab uses simulated data so you can see what changes when you move one control.

Example, step by step

Suppose one job is correctly placed but close to the line. It still incurs a penalty because it is inside the buffer. A job on the wrong side incurs a larger penalty than a job on the same scale just inside the correct side. The control C changes how much the fit cares about these penalties.

What to keep in mind

The chart uses the sign of the score to assign a class. A score of 0.8 does not imply an 80% chance of delay.

How it learns

A linear SVM balances a wide margin against examples that are inside the margin or on the wrong side. The lab uses a linear decision function and approximate subgradient optimization. The class is chosen by the sign of the score. [1]

Equation and terms

The math behind the idea

min ‖w‖²/2 + C · mean(max(0, 1 − yᵢ f(xᵢ))), f(x) = wᵀx + b

w gives the direction perpendicular to the boundary, b shifts it, and yᵢ is −1 or +1. The hinge loss is zero if yᵢ f(xᵢ) is at least 1. C sets the penalty weight. Libraries may use the sum of losses instead of the mean used here. [1] [2]

Try it in the lab

Move the white diamond to both sides of the line and watch the sign of its score. Then compare a low and a high C. The line may move or tilt. At zero you are on the boundary, and the score is not a percentage.

How to read the chart

The sliders place the white diamond on the chart. Move one slider at a time. The horizontal axis shows open items and the vertical axis shows delivery delay. The controls express both measurements on a 0–1 scale, with 0 meaning low and 1 meaning high. The colored background tells you which label the model would assign at that spot.

Lab

Use and evaluate

Scale numeric features and choose C with validation. A linear boundary is a useful baseline before exploring kernels. The score is signed, with a cutoff at zero. Its magnitude does not give a probability. [2]

Review

What happens when a correctly classified point lies inside the margin?

It still incurs hinge loss because its signed margin is less than 1.
