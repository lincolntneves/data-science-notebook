How it learns

KNN keeps the training examples. For a new task, it ranks examples by distance, takes the closest k, and counts how many finished late. The lab uses Euclidean distance and equal votes. [1]

Equation and terms

ŝ(x) = (1/k) ∑ yᵢ, i ∈ Nₖ(x)

Nₖ(x) is the set of k nearest examples to the new task x. Each yᵢ is 1 for late and 0 for on time. ŝ is the fraction that voted late. With three late tasks out of five, the fraction is 60%. [1] [2]

Lab

Use and evaluate

Use comparable scales before measuring distance. Try a small and a large k with validation data. A small k can follow noise, while a large k can erase local patterns. [2]

Review

Why can a feature measured in minutes dominate one on a 0–1 scale?

Without scaling, its larger numerical differences contribute more to the distance.
